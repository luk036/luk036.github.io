### 🔑 Key Points

In Hyperbolic Geometry, two parallel lines meet outside the null circle. ⭕

Given a line ${\color{steelblue}l}$, there are more than one parallel lines that pass through a point ${\color{salmon}p}$.

Notations: To distinguish with Euclidean geometry, lines are written as capital letters.

---

### 📏 Quadrance and Spread in Hyperbolic/Elliptic Geometry

✨ For efficiency, quadrance and spread can also be written as follows.

📏 The **quadrance** ${\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b})$ between points ${\color{salmon}a}$ and ${\color{salmon}b}$ is:
$${\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b}) \equiv 1 - ({\color{salmon}a} \cdot {\color{salmon}b^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })^2 / ({\color{salmon}a} \cdot {\color{salmon}a^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })({\color{salmon}b} \cdot {\color{salmon}b^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })$$

📐 The **spread** ${\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M})$ between lines ${\color{steelblue}L}$ and ${\color{steelblue}M}$ is
$${\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M} ) \equiv 1 - ({\color{steelblue}L} \cdot {\color{steelblue}M^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })^2 / ({\color{steelblue}L} \cdot {\color{steelblue}L^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })({\color{steelblue}M} \cdot {\color{steelblue}M^{\color{firebrick}\perp} })$$

⚠️ Note: In Hyperbolic Geometry, the quadrance of two points inside the null circle is negative.

---

### ↔️ Relation with Traditional Distance and Angle

📏 Hyperbolic Distance: $${\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b} ) = \sinh^2(d({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b} ) )$$

📐 Hyperbolic Angle: $${\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M} ) = \sin^2(\theta({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M}))$$

📏 Elliptic Distance: $${\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b} ) = \sin^2(d({\color{salmon}a}, {\color{salmon}b} ))$$

📐 Elliptic Angle: $${\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M} ) = \sin^2(\theta({\color{steelblue}L}, {\color{steelblue}M}))$$

### 📐 Spread Law

⚖️ Spread Law $$\color{Green}{q_1/S_1 = q_2/S_2 = q_3/S_3}.$$

📐 (Compare with the sine law in traditional Hyperbolic Geometry):
$$\color{red}{\sinh d_1/\sin \theta_1 = \sinh d_2/\sin \theta_2 = \sinh d_3/\sin \theta_3}.$$

📐 (Compare with the sine law in traditional Elliptic Geometry):
$$\color{red}{\sin d_1/\sin \theta_1 = \sin d_2/\sin \theta_2 = \sin d_3/\sin \theta_3}.$$

### 🔢 Triple Formulae

🧮 Theorem (Triple quad formula): If ${\color{salmon}a_1}$, ${\color{salmon}a_2}$ and ${\color{salmon}a_3}$ are collinear points then
$$({\color{salmon}q_1} + {\color{salmon}q_2} + {\color{salmon}q_3})^2 = 2({\color{salmon}q_1}^2 + {\color{salmon}q_2}^2 + {\color{salmon}q_3}^2) + 4 {\color{salmon}q_1} {\color{salmon}q_2} {\color{salmon}q_3}$$

🧮 Theorem (Triple spread formula): If ${\color{steelblue}L_1}$, ${\color{steelblue}L_2}$ and ${\color{steelblue}L_3}$ are concurrent lines then
$$({\color{Green}S_1} + {\color{Green}S_2} + {\color{Green}S_3})^2 = 2({\color{Green}S_1}^2 + {\color{Green}S_2}^2 + {\color{Green}S_3}^2) + 4 {\color{Green}S_1} {\color{Green}S_2} {\color{Green}S_3}.$$

---

### ✖️ Cross Law

✖️ Theorem (Cross law)
$$({\color{Green}S_1} {\color{Green}S_2} {\color{salmon}q_3} - ({\color{Green}S_1} + {\color{Green}S_2} + {\color{Green}S_3}) + 2)^2 = 4(1 - {\color{Green}S_1})(1 - {\color{Green}S_2})(1 - {\color{Green}S_3}).$$

✖️ Theorem (Cross dual law)
$$({\color{salmon}q_1} {\color{salmon}q_2} {\color{Green}S_3} - ({\color{salmon}q_1} + {\color{salmon}q_2} + {\color{salmon}q_3}) + 2)^2 = 4(1 - {\color{salmon}q_1})(1 - {\color{salmon}q_2})(1 - {\color{salmon}q_3}).$$

🔄 Note: Given three quadrances, three spreads can be uniquely determined. Same as Euclidean Geometry.

⚠️ Note: Given three spreads, three quadrances can be uniquely determined. Not true in Euclidean Geometry.

---

### 📐 Right Triangles and Pythagoras

📏 Theorem (Pythagoras): If ${\color{steelblue}L_1}$ and ${\color{steelblue}L_2}$ are perpendicular lines (${\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}L_1}, {\color{steelblue}L_2}) = 1$) then $${\color{salmon}q_3} = {\color{salmon}q_1} + {\color{salmon}q_2} - {\color{salmon}q_1} {\color{salmon}q_2}.$$

📐 Theorem (Thales): Suppose that $\\{\color{salmon}a_1 a_2 a_3\\}$ is a right triangle with ${\color{Green}S_3} = 1$. Then ${\color{Green}S_1} = {\color{salmon}q_1} / {\color{salmon}q_3}$ and ${\color{Green}S_2} = {\color{salmon}q_2} / {\color{salmon}q_3}$.

---

### ➡️ Right Parallax

📐 Theorem (Right parallax): If a right triangle $\\{\color{salmon}a_1 a_2 a_3\\}$ has spreads ${\color{Green}S_1} = 0$, ${\color{Green}S_2} = {\color{Green}S}$ and ${\color{Green}S_3} = 1$, then it will have only one defined quadrance ${\color{salmon}q_1} = {\color{salmon}q}$ given by $${\color{salmon}q} = ({\color{Green}S} - 1)/{\color{Green}S}.$$

🔄 We may restate this result in the form: $${\color{Green}S} = 1 / (1 - {\color{salmon}q}).$$

### 🔺 Triangle Proportions

⚖️ Theorem (Triangle proportions): Suppose that ${\color{salmon}d}$ is a point lying on the line ${\color{steelblue}a_1 a_2}$. Define the quadrances ${\color{royalblue}r_1} \equiv {\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a_1}, {\color{salmon}d})$ and ${\color{royalblue}r_1} \equiv {\color{salmon}q}({\color{salmon}a_2}, {\color{salmon}d})$, and the spreads ${\color{royalblue}R_1} \equiv {\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}a_3 a_1}, {\color{steelblue}a_3 d})$ and ${\color{royalblue}R_2} \equiv {\color{Green}S}({\color{steelblue}a_3 a_2}, {\color{steelblue}a_3 d})$. Then $${\color{royalblue}R_1}/{\color{royalblue}R_2} = ({\color{Green}S_1}/{\color{Green}S_2})({\color{royalblue}r_1}/{\color{royalblue}r_2}) = ({\color{salmon}q_1}/{\color{salmon}q_2})({\color{royalblue}r_1}/{\color{royalblue}r_2}).$$
